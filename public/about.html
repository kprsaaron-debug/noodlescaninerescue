About Us

Isle of Man’s dedicated canine search and rescue charity • Charity No. 1387

Our Story

Noodle’s Canine Rescue began in June 2025 when a group of passionate volunteers came together after a dog named Elvis had been missing for five weeks. Recognising the need for a more coordinated, technology-enabled response to missing dog incidents on the Island, the team formed with a clear mission: to bring lost dogs home faster using a thermal drone, thermal handheld cameras, systematic ground searches, 4g wildlife cameras and trained scent dogs.

Named after Noodle — our own young collie who is currently in scent discrimination training — the organisation has grown rapidly from a grassroots volunteer effort into an officially registered Isle of Man charity (No. 1387) on 4 June 2026. Between 28 May 2025 and 8 November 2026 we logged 39 missing dog searches.

Our Aim & Purpose

Our purpose is simple but vital: to reduce the time dogs spend missing, minimise distress to animals and families, and return every dog safely home. We exist to provide a professional, compassionate and rapid response when a beloved dog goes missing.

Key Achievements

  • Drone capability — Between 28 May 2025 and 8 November 2026 the drone was deployed on 21 of the 39 searches we logged (54%, just over half), and it located four dogs directly: Bruce, Scooby, Toffee and Millie. The weather appears to be our biggest set back for drone deployment, so in the future we look to fund a weatherproof drone to allow for more deployments. The drone has also been invaluable with clearing large areas very quickly with its skilled pilots and observers, allowing us to narrow land based searches down.
  • Official Charity Registration — Registered as Charity No. 1387 on 4 June 2026.
  • Community collaboration — Worked alongside landowners and public volunteers on major searches.
  • Equipment & capability growth — Professional thermal drone now operational, supported by community fundraising, plus 4G wildlife cameras and safety equipment.

Training & Standards

All team members receive structured, role-specific training. This includes search techniques, map reading and navigation (using OS maps and GPS), radio communications protocols, thermal drone operation and image interpretation, canine first aid, and safe working practices in varied Isle of Man terrain (coastal cliffs, plantations, bogs and rural areas).

We maintain the highest standards of safety, animal welfare and professionalism. Our volunteers operate under clear incident command structures. Drone pilots hold appropriate aviation certifications and we continuously invest in advanced training and equipment maintenance.

Our Teams

Drone Team

Our Drone Team operates a professional-grade thermal imaging drone, the DJI Matrice 4T, capable of detecting heat signatures from significant distances, even in dense woodland, coastal areas or complete darkness.

Key capabilities include rapid deployment for night searches, large-area coverage that would take ground teams many hours, and real-time coordination with ground and scent dog teams via radio. Pilots are trained in CAA-equivalent regulations, thermal image interpretation specific to canine detection, search grid planning, and safe flying in the unique Manx environment.

Ground Search Team

Our Ground Search Team carries out systematic physical searches on foot. Volunteers will be trained in proven search patterns, navigation, terrain assessment and radio discipline. They are experienced in the Island’s varied and sometimes hazardous landscapes — from coastal paths and cliffs to plantations, ravines and boggy moorland.

The team works in close coordination with drone intelligence, often being directed to precise locations identified from the air. All members receive canine first aid training and prioritise both human and dog safety during searches.

Dog Handling & Scent Dogs

Our Dog Handling team is just starting out, working with a training scent dog to provide precise location capability, especially in dense cover or when visual/thermal detection is limited. The team is Noodle, currently, our 18-month-old collie who is currently undergoing scent discrimination training to become a fully qualified search dog for the organisation.

Handlers are experienced in working dog training, welfare and integration with drone and ground assets. Scent dogs will add a vital layer of capability — able to detect and follow scent in conditions where technology alone may be challenged — while always operating with the highest standards of animal welfare. We will also be looking to expand our canine scent discrimination team once we have built and proven the training packs with real world searches.
